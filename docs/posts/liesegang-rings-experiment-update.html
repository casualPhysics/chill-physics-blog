Liesegang Rings Experiment Update

4 September 2026 · Precipitates formed but no banding.

In my last post I wrote about my experiment to create Liesegang rings. There, I tried to create geometrically spaced rings with an agar jelly mixed with magnesium chloride hexahydrate, soaked with sodium hydroxide.

I was trying to follow the procedure in Chopard 1999 - the difference is that I used agar jelly instead of the cross-linked PVA paper that they used in the paper. Otherwise, the concentrations of magnesium chloride and sodium hydroxide were kept the same. (Note - I didn't say the difference between agar and PVA in the last post, and it will be updated accordingly, this is my bad).

In the spirit of transparency, I wanted to show the results of the experiment after 9 days of leaving it in my house in open air. To the best of my knowledge the temperature ranged from around 20C to 25C.

In the images below, you can see the precipitate forming, but it doesn't form clean bands like in the Chopard 1999 paper. The precipitate is concentrated at the top, and seems to get more dispersed towards the bottom - but there is no clear separation of bands. So something is happening, but it's not the effect I want. Also, the density of the precipitate seemed to vary in the two tubes

I'm keeping the third tube undisturbed right now - I'm not even picking it up to take a photo. Will see what happens.

What went wrong? I don't know.

  • Biggest thing is that it could be just using the agar jelly instead of PVA.

    • In the same vein I just used tap water, of which the hardness of the water may have had an effect, maybe because of ions from the minerals.

  • One suspicion I have is that I messed up the concentrations because I wasn't looking at the numbers in the paper correctly - I got some help from GPT Sol so it could just be a hallucination. If so this would be a costly mistake and I need to go through it again.

    • I would like to go through the theories behind reaction diffusion systems so I can be more confident that the concentrations should translate with agar jelly.

  • Maybe it was because I was handling the tubes after I mixed everything together, causing the liquid to slosh around.

    • Maybe the table that I put it on wasn't stable or something?

  • Maybe it was the temperature variation in the room, although this seems less likely.
